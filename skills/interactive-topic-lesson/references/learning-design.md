# Design a topic learning and practice experience

The unit of quality is whether the activity supports the stated learning need, not whether a webpage runs or a short quiz can be completed. Use the structure below as design decisions, not fixed page layouts, stage counts, minute targets or problem quotas.

## Choose support that fits the learning goal

Do not infer prior knowledge solely from grade. In method-practice tasks, provide a concise, usable strategy and optional worked help; returning learners can act immediately. In discovery tasks, clarify the goal and allowed operations while leaving the target relationship for the learner to investigate. Give enough information to avoid guessing hidden criteria without turning every lesson into an explanation-first sequence. Keep support available, and distinguish general guidance from item-specific help. A shortcut must have an appropriate scope; do not promote a rule valid in one example into a universal method.

## Plan coverage before authoring questions

Use learningDesign at the lesson root (private author metadata; see contract.md). Map each relevant confusion to a discriminating contrast, a concrete operation, and independently interpretable new-example checks. Derive coverage from the user's need and the topic. Do not add unrelated difficulties merely to make a course look larger.

Derive the contrasts from the topic and reported difficulty rather than a reusable list of question types. Change the relevant conditions, representation, scope or evidence so that learners must distinguish relationships rather than recognize a repeated surface pattern. State the omitted scope instead of implying exhaustive coverage.

A single demonstration and a renamed copy do not establish coverage of distinct confusions. A question can contribute to several dimensions only if the learner's observable actions distinguish them. Do not map one opaque right/wrong choice to many independent findings. A particular wrong choice is evidence consistent with a confusion, not proof of why the child chose it; seek another contrast or a reason/evidence selection before making a stronger interpretation.

Create plausible options using the selected subject guide. Distinguish what each response would show about the target relationship, condition or meaning. Similar choices should require thinking; avoid making the right answer consistently the longest, most polished or the only option that uses the topic words. Ensure there is a defensible unique answer when using single-choice grading; accept or leave open other valid expressions.

### Audit the material and the lure, not only the answer

Before accepting a training item, review the source material as well as its options. Record this audit in the existing subject-review evidence; no extra parent prompt or fixed word count is needed.

- Identify what makes a plausible wrong response attractive and the exact relationship or evidence that distinguishes it. If it is wrong merely because it is obviously irrelevant, absurd, unusually short, or lacks a formulaic keyword, it does not test a subtle distinction. Rewrite weak items before relying on them for training coverage. A later item must require the targeted reasoning beyond an easy entry demonstration.
- Source material must contain enough relevant information or conditions to support the intended distinction. An isolated, obviously irrelevant extra beside a bare correct pattern can be a useful entry example, but cannot by itself support claims about a harder contrast. Add meaningful complexity where needed, not filler, obscure vocabulary or arbitrary length.
- Inspect how the task representation affects difficulty. If its labels, units or layout already perform the classification or reasoning that the learner is meant to do, revise the representation or narrow the claimed learning evidence. Preserve defensible alternatives rather than forcing one response because it matches an author template.
- Check for shortcuts without reading the passage: answer length, repeated wording from the method, obvious labels such as “核心/细节”, ordering, color or highlights that reveal the key. Remove these cues in the independent check. Exploration can provide help, but performance after such help cannot establish unaided distinction.
- Preserve clarity of language and a defensible answer. The intended challenge comes from comparing relevant information, not obscure vocabulary, confusing instructions or options that are equally valid. Where another choice is reasonably defensible, refine the question's requested scope, accept alternatives, or leave it for human review.

For each claimed difficult contrast, the reviewer should be able to explain both why a learner might choose the near miss and why it does not satisfy this task. This is an authored plausibility judgment, not proof a real student will be confused. Learner observations can show an item is easier or harder than expected and should guide revision; never report empirical difficulty from the author's guess.

## Progression and enough evidence

Choose a useful progression: a low-load entry can check the method, then introduce the relevant borderline cases and combine them in new contexts. Increase the reasoning demand through changed structure, partial correctness, less obvious evidence or reduced support; more words or bigger numbers alone are not progression. The material should support the target distinction at the requested level, without making every case trivial or adding irrelevant complexity.

Let learners revisit a needed example or try a related case after an error. Content-based branching can be helpful when it has real follow-up material; do not claim adaptive teaching because only button colors or a score changed. Stages and questions can be split or grouped to fit the goal. No fixed duration, fixed question count, perfect-score gate or forced sequence is required. Balance adequate coverage with meaningful work rather than repetitions to fill time.

## Explain the chosen error

Prepare feedback for every selectable answer and meaningful constructed error before treating the exercise as complete. For a wrong choice:

1. Identify what it actually says or which part is valid, when applicable.
2. Explain the mismatch, omission, unsupported step or wrong focus with the current text/diagram/condition.
3. Give one concrete way to revise or test it. A later optional full explanation can show the answer and compare alternatives.

Make the explanation specific to the observed response and its evidence. A generic request to look again does not explain a mistake. Feedback can support revision without immediately revealing the exact answer; concise feedback still needs a reason, not only a direction toward the key.

Relate correctness to the learning goal too: name the relationship, condition or meaning that makes the response suitable. Avoid praise or correctness marks as the entire feedback. If the design displays evidence, it must relate to the actual response and support the intended reasoning rather than perform it for the learner.

## Report what this practice supports

Keep the first checked response, use of item-specific hints, and responses after explanation/retries distinguishable in page memory. Do not erase history by offering “try again” and then describe a corrected answer as a fresh independent success. An optional general strategy is different from an item-specific hint; state the help actually provided. Opening a hint means help was shown, not proof it was read. Do not claim to measure hidden reasoning.

Give a short learner/parent summary by the covered dimensions: what was attempted, what was difficult on this set, what was corrected after help, and a useful next practice direction. Describe only the distinctions actually examined, rather than diagnosing a lasting weakness. A high total score or a few easy successes does not establish mastery; list untested areas and limited evidence honestly. Pure exploration may report observations and narrower scope without pretending to be a comprehensive training assessment. No authoritative student record, durable tracking or standardized diagnosis is provided by this skill.

## Acceptance before delivery

Review the strategy's applicability, the coverage map against actual tasks, the reasoning progression, every option's feedback, and the summary's match to recorded evidence. Exercise materially different wrong choices where available: they must receive relevant explanations rather than a shared redirection toward the correct answer. Try a hint then a correction, a repeated submission, and a reset; the final summary must not call these new independent successes. Inspect the weakest/most ambiguous item, not only the easiest successful path. Record the performed teaching review as subject evidence in review.json. Static schema checks cannot certify these teaching properties, and actual effectiveness still requires learner observation.

# Mathematics: conditions, representations and reasoning

Load for subject:math after the shared teaching and learning-design references. This guide owns mathematical correctness and learning evidence; the common contract, interaction and LAN-delivery rules remain unchanged. It does not prescribe one visual model, formula sequence or number of questions for every topic.

## Identify the mathematical action

Separate calculation, identifying relevant conditions, interpreting a representation, choosing a model, testing a conjecture and justifying a relationship. Use the actual reported difficulty. If the learner can calculate but chooses the wrong relationship, repeating arithmetic alone misses the need. If a basic operation is genuinely uncertain, offer an accessible check or worked support without inventing a diagnosis.

Record the relevant quantities, units, given conditions and allowed changes before designing a representation. Decide which relationship the learner is meant to notice or use. For practising a known method, make its applicability clear. For discovery, provide a manipulable object and a clear question without revealing the conjecture in advance.

## Keep the representation mathematically faithful

Derive diagrams, labels, calculations and judgments from the same values, and independently recompute results. Preserve geometric proportions where they carry meaning. A decorative appearance or variable named correct is not mathematical evidence.

Use the conventions the topic needs. For example, fractions require equal parts and a specified whole; angles depend on ray directions rather than the drawn lengths; a right angle needs an explicit condition or a representation that actually constructs it. Distinguish lengths from areas, include units, and do not infer physical dimensions from an uncalibrated image. Apply these only when relevant to the chosen topic.

Allowed inputs must preserve the object's conditions. Handle limits and invalid values explicitly rather than silently producing impossible figures. Fully validate numeric inputs instead of accepting a prefix such as 18abc. If equivalent forms are accepted, define equivalence for this task; do not normalize away a unit, sign or representation distinction the learner is being asked to understand.

## Use variation to expose the relationship

Choose operations such as constructing, comparing, changing a quantity, partitioning, reordering, estimating or matching representations for a reason. A controlled change should make its consequences and any invariant observable. Linked representations can help, but unnecessary displays can distract or reveal the answer.

Build the coverage map from plausible mathematical confusions: a missed condition, a wrong quantity, a changed whole, an incomplete boundary, an inappropriate operation or an error in calculation, as appropriate to the topic. A wrong number can arise through different routes; use a choice of method, an intermediate step or another contrast when the reason matters. Do not diagnose the process from the final number alone.

Use near misses that would make sense under a different condition, not arbitrary wrong numbers. A correct numerical answer with an incorrect explanation is not evidence that the intended reasoning is secure. Change structure, representation or conditions for transfer rather than merely replacing numbers. A boundary case can be valuable when it examines the same relationship at the learner's level; do not inflate complexity with unrelated prerequisite demands.

## Feedback and review

Explain the step or relationship actually selected: what quantity was found, which condition was missed or why a representation fails, then offer one concrete correction or comparison. Show a concise worked path when useful or requested. For a correct response, connect the result to the condition and relationship, not only a formula name.

In review.json subject evidence, recompute every answer and inspect the implemented quantities and diagrams, including relevant limits. Test materially different errors and a new representation/context. Verify that the displayed feedback corresponds to actual learner changes and that the learning summary does not convert a corrected result into independent success.

## Evaluation boundary

The canvas can support constructions and multi-step reasoning, but the formal-check player currently grades single choices. Selecting a valid argument does not by itself verify an independently written proof. Complex symbolic equivalence, free-form proofs or geometry beyond the implemented model need an actual evaluator or human review. If those are essential to the requested outcome, explain the gap and let the user choose scope or support before substituting a simpler question or claiming the stronger outcome. Do not invent a numerical tolerance or correctness judgment merely to accept an answer.

# Chinese: text, meaning and expression

Load for subject:chinese after the shared teaching and learning-design references. This guide owns Chinese content decisions; the common contract, interaction and LAN-delivery rules remain unchanged. It is not a fixed lesson template, a complete curriculum or a rule that all Chinese learning is paragraph-summary practice.

## Identify the language task

Distinguish the intended action: interpreting a word in context, relating sentences, finding evidence, identifying a main idea, appreciating an expression, revising a sentence, or producing one's own wording. Choose the focus reported by the user. Do not expand a reading difficulty into a generic full-course test or infer decoding, vocabulary or writing weakness from one summary error.

Use supplied grade and experience to set reading load, but do not invent textbook alignment. Preserve an inspected source's meaning and wording where the task depends on them. Original supplementary texts need a coherent communicative purpose, not just sentences engineered around the correct option. If a task depends on a specific unseen passage, textbook definition or expected answer, obtain that material instead of inventing it.

## Choose support and operations

Give an actionable criterion when practising a method; for discovery or interpretation, let a useful comparison precede a generalization. Support can include comparing expressions, changing a word, reorganizing information, selecting evidence, or trying a concise formulation. Select the operation for the learning goal rather than always using answer cards.

Topic-specific examples of design decisions, not compulsory activities:

| Focus | Useful distinction | What counts as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Word meaning | Different senses supported by different contexts | The surrounding words that make a sense fit |
| Sentence relationship | Cause, contrast, sequence or another actual relation | How changing a connection changes the meaning |
| Main idea | A locally true statement vs an appropriately scoped account | Information needed to preserve the text's central relation |
| Expression/revision | A wording that changes emphasis vs one that changes or loses meaning | The intended meaning and effect in this context |

Do not generalize a method beyond its scope. A narrative may be summarized through important events and outcomes; a description or explanation may need characteristics or relationships. A central sentence can be useful evidence when present, but location alone is not a rule for finding the main idea. Highlighting may help examine evidence; it must not automatically perform a task whose goal is for the learner to locate that evidence.

## Author meaningful material and alternatives

For information-selection tasks, choose texts with enough connected information to support the desired distinctions. Relevant secondary actions, explanatory background and partial accounts can make a near miss plausible. A clearly decorative detail in an otherwise bare text can introduce a distinction, but should not be the only basis for claiming training coverage. Length is chosen for reasoning and reading load, without fixed character quotas or filler.

Before accepting an option, explain why it is plausible and why it fits or fails this question's requested scope. A true statement can still be too narrow, too broad, focus on a different issue or add an unsupported inference. The text must decide between these interpretations. Avoid making the best summary consistently the longest or the only option with a memorized formula. Do not count one opaque correct choice as proof the learner can distinguish every listed error type.

Meaning-preserving omissions and reasonable extra context may both be defensible. Concision is not a rule to delete every description, every example or every background condition; its function depends on the text and task. Choose a defensible unique answer for single-choice questions. If alternatives are valid, refine the requested scope, accept multiple expressions in the activity or retain uncertainty for human review.

## Feedback and review

Explain the actual response with textual evidence: what it preserves, what relationship or meaning it misses, and a concrete revision move. Different partial readings need different explanations. Showing only the correct response's supporting words or asking a shared leading question is not selected-error analysis. For a correct response, explain how it meets the criterion without implying it is the only possible wording.

In review.json subject evidence, verify each interpretation against the inspected/original passage, compare the most plausible competing answers, and examine a materially different new text. Record where the interaction shows recognition or supported revision rather than independent expression. Hint-assisted corrections and a few simple successes remain limited evidence about this set.

## Evaluation boundary

The canvas can collect an open formulation or let learners revise text, but nonempty input or an exact-string match does not evaluate its meaning. The current formal-check player grades single choices. Selecting a supplied summary does not establish that a learner can independently write one, and it cannot establish broad composition quality. If the requested outcome requires reliable automatic evaluation of unrestricted expression or recitation not supported by the host, explain the missing capability and let the user choose a narrower target, human review or a separately supported evaluator. Do not silently substitute recognition and report the original production goal as achieved.

# English: form, meaning, context and language skill

Load for subject:english after the shared teaching and learning-design references, including when instructions and explanations are in Chinese. This guide owns English content and evidence of language performance; the common contract, interaction and LAN-delivery rules remain unchanged. It is not a fixed grammar lesson, translation drill or audio requirement.

## Identify what language ability is being practised

Specify the target: vocabulary in context, a form–meaning distinction, reading comprehension, written expression, listening or spoken interaction. Grade does not establish vocabulary, textbook sequence or prior exposure; use supplied information and ask only for missing material that would change the task. Keep unfamiliar background vocabulary from becoming an unintended test of a different skill.

Define what success would show. Choosing a word, understanding a short sentence and independently producing that sentence are different outcomes. A grammar label alone is not a learner action. Establish the intended meaning, speaker situation or visual context before deciding how to practise a form.

## Keep form connected to meaning

Use short, natural contexts at the requested level. Explanations can use the learner's preferred language; the target English should remain clear. Provide a concise cue or worked comparison for method practice, or let the learner compare contexts when noticing a pattern is the goal. Do not universalize a rule beyond the construction actually taught.

Words can have several senses and expressions can have valid alternatives. Choose the sense supported by this context rather than treating one translation as the word's entire meaning. For grammar, test the intended contrast, such as reference, time, number or communicative function, instead of rewarding keyword matching alone. Do not assume a form has one function in every construction or brand a valid alternative variety or wording as wrong without a task-relevant reason.

When images support language meaning, make the reference objects and spatial or temporal relationships unambiguous. Their perspective, occlusion and labels must not create a second valid answer. A picture that is hard to interpret is not evidence that the child lacks the corresponding word or sentence form.

## Choose operations and plausible contrasts

Possible operations include changing a scene, matching meaning to a sentence, ordering or editing a sentence, comparing contexts, completing a short exchange, or producing a response. Choose among them for the target ability; do not turn every task into selecting a translation.

Keep distractors plausible while ensuring the intended contrast decides the answer. A sentence may be grammatically formed but unsuitable for this situation; another may express the desired meaning with a form error. Isolate the reported confusion when introducing it, then combine relevant distinctions or reduce cues for a new case. Do not mark an alternative wrong only because it is absent from the author's preferred phrasing.

For typed responses, accept equivalent casing, punctuation, contractions or expressions when they are irrelevant to the target. Preserve distinctions the task is actually checking, such as a number marker or spelling contrast. Nonempty text is not evidence of a correct or meaningful utterance. Open responses with defensible alternatives may require human review instead of an artificial exact-string key.

## Feedback and review

Explain the selected response using both its form and its meaning in the actual situation. Distinguish a grammatical error from a context mismatch; offer a small edit, comparison or new context that lets the learner try the relationship again. A correction without its relevant reason is insufficient. Correct feedback should say why the expression fits this situation rather than only praise it.

In review.json subject evidence, check every English option for naturalness, context fit and competing valid interpretations. Examine the least obvious contrast, not only the entry match. Verify that audio, picture or translation cues do not silently supply the skill being assessed. Summaries should name the distinction and modality actually observed, and keep helped/retried responses separate from first checks.

## Audio and evaluation boundary

Respect a request for silence. Text and touch operations must remain usable when no audio service exists. Use listening or speaking functionality only when the actual environment/host supplies working media or evaluation and the learner has authorized the relevant use; do not present unavailable TTS, ASR, recording or pronunciation scoring as operational. The canvas cannot make network requests to add a service secretly.

A text-only selection task does not test listening, pronunciation or spontaneous speech. The formal-check player grades single choices, not unrestricted writing or speech quality. If the requested learning outcome depends on a modality or evaluator the host does not have, explain the specific missing capability and let the user choose a narrower outcome or real additional support. Do not silently replace listening with reading or speaking with recognition and claim the original goal was met.
